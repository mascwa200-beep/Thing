Survival guide diagrams — provenance & licensing
=================================================
The knot illustrations in this folder are scanned engravings from the
1911 Encyclopaedia Britannica (11th edition), which is in the PUBLIC DOMAIN
worldwide (published 1911). Sourced via Wikimedia Commons.

RE-ENCODING NOTICE (applies to every raster file in this folder and in kb/)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
All raster diagrams are bundled as WebP, re-encoded from the originals at quality 85 and
scaled down where they exceeded 1280 pixels wide. Nothing is cropped, recoloured, overlaid
or otherwise altered: the change is one of resolution and file format only, made so the
corpus fits a phone (68 MB to 31 MB) without changing what the reader sees — the app draws
these at 260dp on Android and 620dp on the desktop, so the discarded pixels were never
displayed. Where a licence requires that modifications be indicated, this paragraph is that
indication. Most SVG files are bundled unchanged, as vector; the rest are covered below.

SOME DIAGRAMS THAT WERE ORIGINALLY VECTOR ARE BUNDLED AS WEBP INSTEAD. Any such file was
rendered from the original SVG at 1280 pixels wide and composited onto a white background,
which is the same background the rest of the line art here already carries. As above, no
cropping, recolouring or overlay: only resolution and file format. The Commons "File:"
links recorded below still point at the original vector works. This is stated as a rule
rather than a list so that it stays true as the corpus grows; a file's own entry below
names the vector it came from.

There are two reasons a diagram takes that route. Two of them — circadian-clock.webp and
female-reproductive.webp — were converted because the desktop reader's renderer could not
parse circadian-clock.svg at all (it drew nothing and fell back to a caption) and because
at 981 kB and 456 kB the pair were far heavier than the drawings warranted; as raster they
are 93 kB and 76 kB. The others are vectors that arrived over the 390 kB ceiling this
corpus places on bundled SVG — a limit that exists because a heavy vector can stall a phone
renderer — and which would otherwise have been discarded even though the drawing was the
right one for the page.
